# Single-screen ports: precedents for a controller-only Sims 2 DS

Research notes for the sims2-ds-wide redesign (one screen, controller, no touch). Compiled 2026-11-05 from web sources; every claim carries a link. Points that could not be verified against the cited page are marked **(unverified)**. Quotes are reproduced as found; nothing is paraphrased as a quote.

Scope reminder for our case: town free roam, tab menus, dialogue, social reactions, and the touch minigames (vacuum, cards, painting/drawing, music mixer, metal detector).

---

## Part A — DS and 3DS games moved to a single screen

### A1. Castlevania Dominus Collection (Konami, 2024; Switch/PS/Xbox/PC)

Three DS Castlevanias (Dawn of Sorrow, Portrait of Ruin, Order of Ecclesia) emulated on one screen.

- **Second screen → side panel.** The game occupies the left of the display; the DS lower-screen content (map, stats, bestiary) is drawn on the right. CGMagazine: the collection puts "the action on the left 2/3 of the screen" while "the maps and a bestiary entry use the remaining third, making it easy to check your location and stats at a glance" ([CGMagazine](https://www.cgmagonline.com/review/game/castlevania-dominus-collection-ps5/)). Push Square describes "one main panel on the left of your screen" with two smaller squares on the right that replace "the information that would have been on the DS' lower screen" ([Push Square](https://www.pushsquare.com/reviews/ps5/castlevania-dominus-collection)). Nintendo World Report: "the second screen is simply reproduced on the right side of the Switch or TV screen" ([NWR](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/68577)).
- **Layout options, switchable at any time.** NWR counts "five screen settings" and notes that in handheld mode "the map screen is admittedly quite small by default" ([NWR](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/68577)). Nintendo Everything describes a "triple-screen" mode that splits the top-screen data into two panels beside a larger gameplay view, a "classic DS look", and that "you can swap between modes at any time in the pause menu"; it adds that "the map screen in particular suffers if you use the triple screen option" ([Nintendo Everything](https://nintendoeverything.com/review-castlevania-dominus-collection-switch/)).
- **Touch → virtual cursor on the right stick, trigger to tap.** "The right trigger and analog stick summon a virtual cursor to replicate a finger or stylus on the DS touch screen"; the reviewer found this "pretty natural in practice" for Dawn of Sorrow's block-breaking because "using your controller's right stick is pretty similar to drawing on the touch screen for right-handers" ([CGMagazine](https://www.cgmagonline.com/review/game/castlevania-dominus-collection-ps5/)). NWR: "The touch screen functionality has been relegated to a cursor controlled by the right stick" ([NWR](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/68577)). Nintendo Everything is harsher: "Using the mouse-style cursor feels significantly clunkier" ([Nintendo Everything](https://nintendoeverything.com/review-castlevania-dominus-collection-switch/)). A search summary mentioned three cursor speed options **(unverified; not found on any fetched page)**.
- **Touch-only puzzle → button pattern, original kept as option, plus rewind.** Dawn of Sorrow's boss-ending Magic Seals (traced with the stylus on DS) are now button sequences: "each seal has a pattern of button inputs" and the collection's rewind is "perfect if you flub a magic seal input" ([CGMagazine](https://www.cgmagonline.com/review/game/castlevania-dominus-collection-ps5/)). Nintendo Everything confirms the tracing option remains and "the option to input a combination of buttons has been implemented", calling it "slightly less intuitive, but easier than trying to trace them with your finger after a little practice" ([Nintendo Everything](https://nintendoeverything.com/review-castlevania-dominus-collection-switch/)). Official feature list: "rewind, quick saves and much more!", Touch Screen listed as supported, TV/Tabletop/Handheld modes ([Nintendo store](https://www.nintendo.com/us/store/products/castlevania-dominus-collection-switch/)).
- **Reception.** Praise: "a pretty good solution overall" for the two-screen problem; criticism: the reviewer "caught myself looking at only the map" and wanted "an option to take these extras out of the equation altogether" ([CGMagazine](https://www.cgmagonline.com/review/game/castlevania-dominus-collection-ps5/)). Push Square: pro "Screen layout grows on you", con "Occasionally fiddly adapted touch controls" ([Push Square](https://www.pushsquare.com/reviews/ps5/castlevania-dominus-collection)).

### A2. Etrian Odyssey Origins Collection (Atlus, 2023; Switch/PC)

Map drawing was the DS bottom-screen core loop.

- **Official position.** The Switch store page lists "Touch screen mapping functionality for the Nintendo Switch" and quality-of-life items "difficulty selection and save slots"; mapping is "drawing walls and placing icons to note special events and resources" ([Nintendo store](https://www.nintendo.com/us/store/products/etrian-odyssey-origins-collection-switch/)). The page does not describe the controller path.
- **Controller path exists but is second-best.** Nintendo Life: mapping tools are "utilized via either the touch screen or traditional controls", "the touchscreen feels much more natural and intuitive for mapmaking", while "the traditional controls still offer a decent alternative for when you're playing on the TV" but "feel a bit awkward". An automap can be enabled in settings "to do the majority of the work for you as you walk" ([Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/reviews/switch-eshop/etrian-odyssey-origins-collection)). A reader comment on the same page describes automap levels off/ON/FULL, with FULL still leaving secret passages and chests to place by hand **(reader comment, unverified)**.
- **Where the map went.** Review round-ups report the map drawn into a corner of the main screen and the main view enlarging to full screen in combat; NWR says map-drawing controls "aren't as natural as they were on DS and 3DS" **(search summary of [NWR EO II HD review](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/63843/etrian-odyssey-ii-hd-switch-review) and others; not verified on page)**.
- **Reception.** Nintendo Life calls the set "effectively straight, up-rezzed ports of the DS games" ([Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/reviews/switch-eshop/etrian-odyssey-origins-collection)).

### A3. The World Ends with You: Solo Remix (iOS, 2012) and Final Remix (Switch, 2018)

The DS original ran two simultaneous battles, one per screen. Both re-releases redesigned combat for one screen.

- **Solo Remix (one touch screen).** "Players control Neku with a summoning system that enables the other characters to assist in attack"; "Combat moves are initiated through swipes or taps, just like moving around"; the game "does struggle to tell the difference at times, which gets irritating when it happens repeatedly" ([148Apps](http://www.148apps.com/reviews/world-ends-solo-remix-review/)).
- **Final Remix (Switch).** Official text: "Take control of real-time, action-fueled RPG battles using touch controls or Joy-Con controllers"; Touch Screen listed; "exclusive two-player combat" ([Nintendo store](https://www.nintendo.com/us/store/products/the-world-ends-with-you-final-remix-switch/)). Nintendo Life: combat "has been radically changed since the DS original"; the partner is now "another Pin, called into battle by an input of their own"; docked play uses one Joy-Con "in a Wii remote-like fashion", which is "manageable, but far from intuitive", with the need to "frequently tap a button to recenter the cursor"; verdict "neither feels like a completely satisfying option"; a second player can "pick up a Joy-Con and take control of your partner directly" ([Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/reviews/nintendo-switch/world_ends_with_you_final_remix)). Other headlines: "Great Game, Rough Controls" ([Wccftech](https://wccftech.com/review/the-world-ends-with-you-final-remix/)) and "a so-so port of an excellent jRPG" ([TechRaptor](https://techraptor.net/content/the-world-ends-with-you-final-remix-review)).
- **Lesson.** Pointer emulation as the primary combat input on TV was the most criticised element across reviews.

### A4. Ghost Trick: Phantom Detective (Capcom, 2023; Switch/PS4/Xbox/PC, mobile 2024)

Stylus-driven DS puzzle adventure.

- **Developer statement (primary).** Remaster director Atsushi Maruyama: "The biggest challenge was adjusting the user interface (UI) of the game", because where and how information is shown strongly affects play; showing whether you can "trick" an item after possessing it was "a particularly difficult element"; the team designed "with the player's point of view in mind" ([Destructoid interview](https://www.destructoid.com/ghost-trick-phantom-detective-remaster-interview-feature-capcom/); summarised with names at [GoNintendo](https://www.gonintendo.com/contents/21801-ghost-trick-phantom-detective-devs-on-the-challenge-of-adapting-the-game-for)). A second GoNintendo piece attributes the same quote to Shu Takumi **(attribution conflict; [GoNintendo 21880](https://gonintendo.com/contents/21880-capcom-details-the-challenge-of-adapting-ghost-trick-s-dual-screen-layout-to-a))**.
- **Input.** NWR: you "trace a line, either by using the touch screen similar to the DS-version or by moving the joystick" between your soul and an object's core; "I like some of the UI changes that have been made" ([NWR](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/64148)). Nintendo UK lists "Touch screen features" and Pro Controller support ([Nintendo UK](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/Games/Nintendo-Switch-games/Game-2338699.html)).
- **Aspect ratio kept at 4:3.** RPGFan: the remaster "keeps its 4:3 aspect ratio found on DS" and the reviewer "would have loved to see the world of Ghost Trick through a 16:9 lens" ([RPGFan](https://www.rpgfan.com/review/ghost-trick-phantom-detective-remaster/)).
- **Reception.** Generally positive on the stick-based conversion; the remaster is described as conservative. One quoted line, "the conversion from touch controls to a traditional controller works well", appeared in a search summary without a clear source **(unverified)**.

### A5. Professor Layton: Curious Village HD (mobile, 2018) and Layton's Mystery Journey Deluxe (Switch, 2019)

- **Curious Village HD keeps the two-screen split on a phone.** Kotaku: the screen is "split horizontally into two halves to mirror the DS layout"; animated cutscenes fill the screen in landscape; exploration taps are harder because "clicking precisely on these things with a large human finger is quite different" from a stylus; a tablet was easier ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/professor-layton-on-mobile-could-really-use-a-stylus-o-1829401026)). Nintendo Wire described a portrait display emulating the dual screens **(search summary; [Nintendo Wire](https://nintendowire.com/news/2018/05/29/professor-layton-curious-village-getting-hd-port-smartphones/))**.
- **Mystery Journey Deluxe offers three input methods.** Cubed3: "touchscreen, buttons only, or motion controls"; the d-pad cursor has "only two speeds: fast and slow"; "The best option by far is disconnecting both Joycon and use motion controls"; the memo function "now covers the puzzle explanation" whereas on 3DS both were visible; the game "was designed for a console with two screens" and the redesign "just does not work that well"; still 8/10 ([Cubed3](https://www.cubed3.com/games/reviews/nintendo-switch/laytons-mystery-journey-katrielle-and-the-millionaires-conspiracy-deluxe-edition)). Nintendo Life's reviewer, in comments: "using the sticks to move the cursor is still a perfectly fine alternative" **(reviewer comment, [Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/reviews/nintendo-switch/laytons_mystery_journey_katrielle_and_the_millionaires_conspiracy_-_deluxe_edition))**. Checkpoint Gaming: "Controls don't translate well to Switch" **(search summary; [Checkpoint](https://checkpointgaming.net/reviews/2019/11/laytons-mystery-journey-katrielle-and-the-millionaires-conspiracy-deluxe-edition-review-elementary-my-dear))**.

### A6. Phoenix Wright: Ace Attorney Trilogy (2019) and Apollo Justice: Ace Attorney Trilogy (2024)

- **Phoenix Wright Trilogy.** Hardcore Gamer: the console version takes "a new approach having lost the bottom screen of the Nintendo handhelds"; "The biggest flaw of playing with a controller is the speed of the cursor" when investigating or presenting evidence; "the magnifying glass does inform players where they've already examined"; "The Switch version also supports touch controls in handheld mode" ([Hardcore Gamer](https://hardcoregamer.com/reviews/review-phoenix-wright-ace-attorney-trilogy-console/328566/)). One review describes the pointer as moved by the right stick with a bumper to pan scrolling scenes **(search summary, source not confirmed)**; another wanted "a speedier cursor" ([The Technovore](https://thetechnovore.com/2019/04/12/phoenix-wright-ace-attorney-trilogy-reviewed/), via search summary).
- **Apollo Justice Trilogy (DS/3DS forensics).** RPG Site: the original "played over two screens" and on PC/gamepad "many of the interactions are replaced by simple button presses that leave them feeling a bit vestigial", "a small loss"; convenience features include "automatic text scrolling and a text log" and a hands-off "story mode" ([RPG Site](https://rpgsite.net/review/15365-apollo-justice-ace-attorney-trilogy-eview)). GameGrin: Perceive is used by pressing "the top face button" when prompted; forensics "always feel like fun little minigames" ([GameGrin](https://www.gamegrin.com/reviews/apollo-justice-ace-attorney-trilogy-review/)). NWR lists Story Mode and optional tutorials, 9/10 ([NWR](https://nintendoworldreport.com/review/66077/apollo-justice-ace-attorney-trilogy-switch-review)); Nintendo Life notes the "notes" system and "consult" option ([Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/reviews/nintendo-switch/apollo-justice-ace-attorney-trilogy)). Nintendojo reportedly found the interface "isn't anywhere near as elegant nor precise as it was on DS or 3DS" **(search summary; [Nintendojo](https://www.nintendojo.com/?p=204610))**.

### A7. Ace Attorney Investigations Collection (2024)

- **No touch on Switch; cursor replaces stylus.** Nintendojo: a "touch-less interface" that "works just fine", but a cursor "is simply not as impressive" than the DS stylus; Mind Chess "One wrong move and the game forces a restart"; Little Thief crime-scene recreation "highly entertaining if somewhat underutilized" ([Nintendojo](https://nintendojo.com/reviews/review-ace-attorney-investigations-collection-switch)). A pre-release preview said there was "no touchscreen support as of this writing" **(search summary; [TouchArcade](https://toucharcade.com/2024/08/01/ace-attorney-investigations-collection-steam-deck-nintendo-switch-gameplay-preview-impressions-final-build/))**.
- **Movement and feedback.** NWR: the player "uses the control stick or D-pad to move Miles around a crime scene"; "Visual indicators" show when an element is fully explored; pixel/HD toggle; chapter select ([NWR](https://nintendoworldreport.com/review/68426)). Vooks: "Moving around the world using the control stick and clicking the button on things or people"; Story Mode "can be turned on and off at a whim" ([Vooks](https://vooks.net/ace-attorney-investigations-collection-switch-review)).

### A8. Picross S series and Dr Kawashima's Brain Training (Switch)

- **Picross S (Jupiter).** The first six Switch entries were button-only; touch arrived with S7 in two modes: "Touch Hold makes it so the touchscreen only works when holding a button or stick" and "Touch Toggle makes it toggled on and off when you touch a button or stick"; the reviewer preferred Toggle but found it "not as elegant as it was on DS and 3DS due to the form factor and lack of a built-in stylus" ([NWR Picross S7](https://nintendoworldreport.com/review/59283/picross-s7-switch-review)).
- **Brain Training.** Official page: classic exercises are played "holding your Nintendo Switch console vertically and using a stylus or your finger"; new exercises use "the right Joy-Con's IR Motion Camera"; TV, Tabletop and Handheld modes all listed as supported ([Nintendo AU](https://nintendo.com/au/games/nintendo-switch/dr-kawashimas-brain-training-for-nintendo-switch)). A review states "the majority of the exercises cannot be played while the Switch is docked" **(search summary; [Switchaboo](https://switchaboo.com/dr-kawashimas-brain-training-switch-review))**. This is the opposite approach to ours: it keeps touch and gives up docked play.

### A9. Dragon Quest IV/V/VI (DS remakes → mobile) and Radiant Historia

- The DS remakes went to iOS/Android as single-screen portrait games. An App Store user review: "The game runs in portrait mode and there is no way to run in landscape mode" **(user review on [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/dragon-quest-iv/id847381935))**. Multiplayer.it reportedly calls portrait-only a deliberate choice for one-handed play **(search summary; [Multiplayer.it](https://multiplayer.it/recensioni/136715-dragon-quest-iv-chapters-of-the-chosen-le-cronache-dei-prescelti.html))**. These ports stack the former two screens vertically; no controller precedent.
- Radiant Historia: no single-screen port was identified in this research (Perfect Chronology is 3DS). Not pursued.

### A10. Zelda: Phantom Hourglass / Spirit Tracks on Wii U Virtual Console; Nintendo Switch Online status

- **NSO.** No official Nintendo announcement of DS games on Nintendo Switch Online was found as of 2026-11-05; results were rumours and patent speculation only ([ComicBook](https://comicbook.com/gaming/news/nintendo-ds-games-may-be-coming-to-nintendo-switch-online-soon), [inkl](https://www.inkl.com/news/nintendo-switch-online-patent-reveals-ds-games-coming-next-to-online-service-but-when)). The only confirmed 2026 item is the Phantom Hourglass soundtrack on Nintendo Music ([GamingBible](https://www.gamingbible.com/news/platform/nintendo/legend-of-zelda-phantom-hourglass-nintendo-music-041382-20260120)).
- **Wii U Virtual Console layouts (official).** "During gameplay, press the ZR Button to suspend the game and display the Virtual Console menu. Select Screen Settings. Select the screen layout you would like to use" ([Nintendo Support](https://en-americas-support.nintendo.com/app/answers/detail/a_id/14642)). Launch coverage: "Some games will use your TV monitor as the main screen, while others will use both screens as one connected screen. Players can select a layout that best suits that title" ([Gematsu](https://www.gematsu.com/2015/04/nintendo-64-and-ds-games-hit-wii-u-today)). Nintendo Life counts "a total of six changeable screen layouts", including one that "requires the Wii U GamePad to be held vertically in a portrait mode fashion" ([Nintendo Life, WarioWare Touched](https://www.nintendolife.com/reviews/wiiu-eshop/warioware_touched_ds)).
- **Spirit Tracks on Wii U.** "areas that require constant referencing of the map aren't very well suited to having the bottom screen on the GamePad"; the virtual-DS view is "a tad silly in practice"; the vertical GamePad is "an ergonomic nightmare"; the microphone "seems to struggle to pick up blowing inputs when the controller's being held vertically"; overall "fiddly compromise" ([Nintendo Life, Spirit Tracks Wii U](https://www.nintendolife.com/games/ds/legend_of_zelda_spirit_tracks/review)). Touch itself was not remapped to buttons; the GamePad touchscreen stood in for the DS screen.

### A11. 3DS → Switch remakes where the lower screen mattered

- **Luigi's Mansion 2 HD (2024).** The map moved from the 3DS bottom screen to a corner overlay: "moved up from the bottom screen on the 3DS version to the top-right corner" ([VGC](https://videogameschronicle.com/review/luigis-mansion-2-hd)); "a minimap is now overlaid in the upper right-hand corner" ([Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/140350/luigis-mansion-2-hd-remaster-review-score)); it "can be maximized with the push of the '-' button" and fades while the Poltergust is in use ([My Nintendo News](https://mynintendonews.com/2024/06/11/preview-luigis-mansion-2-hd/)); the game "has lost nothing in the transition to a new single-screen experience outside of that quality-of-life aspect" ([Player2](https://www.player2.net.au/2024/06/luigis-mansion-2-hd-hands-on-preview/)). **(All four from search summaries; quotes not re-verified on page.)**
- **Another Code: Recollection (2024).** The remake removed the DS stylus puzzles rather than mapping them: it "took the puzzle design back to the drawing board" ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/gaming/another-code-recollection-review)); it "no longer depends on DS touch or the Wiimote for puzzles" ([Vooks](https://vooks.net/another-code-recollection-review)); it ignores "the dual Joy-Cons, gyro, and touch screen" ([CGM](https://www.cgmagonline.com/review/game/another-code-recollection-review)). **(Search summaries.)**
- **Bravely Default Flying Fairy HD Remaster (Switch 2, 2025).** Two new minigames use Joy-Con 2 mouse mode; Kotaku found them "surprisingly very intuitive" ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/nintendo-switch-2-mouse-controls-joycon-bravely-default-1851779935)); TechRadar: "little more than a tech demo" and "hidden in submenus, these will be forgotten almost immediately" ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/gaming/nintendo/bravely-default-flying-fairy-hd-remaster-review)). **(Search summaries.)** Relevant if we ever target Switch 2 mouse input for painting.

### A12. Community precedent: emulator layouts and Pokémon FullDex

- **melonDS (desktop).** FAQ: the "Natural" layout "keeps them stacked like an actual DS"; other options keep screens "aligned horizontally or vertically regardless of rotation"; sizing can emphasise one screen while the other stays at native resolution, with "Auto" choosing per game ([melonDS FAQ](https://melonds.kuribo64.net/faq.php)). Release 0.9.2 added "new screen modes" and a "hotkey for swapping screens" ([0.9.2](https://github.com/melonDS-emu/melonDS/releases/tag/0.9.2)); 0.9.3 mentions "Fix touchscreen code in non-hybrid layout mode", confirming a hybrid layout exists ([0.9.3](https://github.com/melonDS-emu/melonDS/releases/tag/0.9.3)).
- **melonDS DS (libretro).** Right stick = "Move Virtual Cursor", R3 = "Touch Virtual Cursor", R2 = "Next Screen Layout", L2 = Microphone; supports "sideways rotation" and a configurable sequence of layouts to cycle ([libretro docs](https://docs.libretro.com/library/melonds_ds/)).
- **DeSmuME (libretro).** Layouts: "bottom/top, left/right, right/left, top only, bottom only, quick switch, hybrid/top, hybrid/bottom"; "Hybrid layout scale" 1 or 3; "Show both screens" to drop the small screen; pointer modes for right/left analog ("none, emulated, absolute, pressed"); R2 = "Tap Stylus" ([libretro docs](https://docs.libretro.com/library/desmume/)).
- **Pokémon FullDex** ([README](https://github.com/mamesj/pokemon-fulldex)). Merged view: the two DS screens "become one 16:9 picture", with the touch screen's buttons "tucked beside the action"; SCREEN LAYOUT offers MERGED or a conventional two-screen mode. Battle buttons: in Black/White (2) the FIGHT/BAG/RUN/POKÉMON panel sits beside the battle (PANEL) or as large buttons in the message box (STRIP). Platinum's Pokétch "isn't shown during normal play" and is one press of the screen-swap button away; the X menu sits at the right edge. On Switch docked, the right stick moves a cursor and ZL touches; pressing the right stick shows the other screen; handheld uses the touchscreen. Settings include STICK TOUCH CURSOR, and any DS button, screen swap and fast-forward can be rebound.

## Part B — The Sims on single-screen consoles, 2003–2007

Official manuals (OCR scans on archive.org) are the primary sources; button glyphs are partly garbled in the OCR, so face-button names are given only where legible.

### B1. The Sims Bustin' Out (PS2/Xbox/GC, 2003)

Source: [PS2 manual](https://archive.org/details/ps2_Sims_Bustin_Out_The_USA) (OCR); [Xbox manual scan](https://archive.org/details/xboxmanual_The_Sims_Bustin_Out) (no OCR text).

- **Control model.** Cursor-based: "Move Cursor" on the left analog stick, Select/Cancel Action on face buttons, "Follow Sim" on a third face button, "Change Sim" on L2/R2, "Edit/Cancel Action" on SELECT. Environment: right stick "Zoom/Rotate", L1/R1 "Change game speed", a face button for "Walls up/down".
- **Panels on the d-pad.** "D-button left opens the Career panel, right opens Relationship, down opens Motives, and up opens Personality"; START opens the Modes menu (Buy and Build). Seven motives listed: Hunger, Energy, Social, Comfort, Bladder, Fun, Room. HUD shows a Mood Meter, time of day and Simoleons.
- **Buy/Build.** Catalogue by item type; L1/R1 rotate an object; press the left stick to activate a grab cursor; walls are drawn by confirming a start point, moving the stick along the line and confirming again.
- **Modes.** "Bust Out" is level-based with goals and unlocks; Free Play has "no established goals" and unlocks after entering Mom's house. Two-player requires a second controller and two memory cards.
- **Context menu praised.** The Xbox version pops a menu "with the fridge and the name of the person standing near it" when you click in a crowded area, so you need not aim at one object; the reviewer called this their "favorite change" and the game "much more streamlined" ([Gaming Nexus](https://www.gamingnexus.com/Article/439/The-Sims-Bustin-Out)). Build mode was the common complaint: controls "are usually fine and simple, but Build mode falls apart" **(search summary; [Game Revolution](https://www.gamerevolution.com/review/35994-the-sims-bustin-out-review))**. Era context from the first console Sims: "the Xbox version's interface is much simpler and easier to use" than the PC's ([GameVortex, The Sims Xbox](https://ps2.psillustrated.com/gamevortex/soft_rev.php/988)).

### B2. The Sims 2 (PS2/GameCube/Xbox, 2005)

Source: [PS2 manual](https://archive.org/details/ps2_Sims_2_The_USA) (OCR via [stream](https://archive.org/stream/ps2_Sims_2_The_USA/Sims_2_The_USA_djvu.txt)).

- **Two control styles, toggled with SELECT.** "Direct Control": third-person, left stick moves the Sim; position near an object or person and press the action button. "Classic Control": a cursor moved with the left stick, with a snap-to-Sim button. Right stick zooms/rotates the camera; L1/R1 "Freeze action / fast forward"; L2/R2 switch to next/previous Sim; START pause menu.
- **HUD.** Mood meter (green happy, red unhappy); Needs, Skills and Wants-and-Fears panels reached through the D-pad; time of day and Simoleons on screen. Needs: Social, Bladder, Hygiene, Hunger, Fun, Energy, Comfort, Room.
- **Wants/Fears/Aspiration.** Each Sim has an Aspiration driving Wants and Fears; fulfilling Wants earns Aspiration points that unlock objects; Fears lower the meter; "Gold Wants advance you through Story mode". A review adds "Platinum wants unlock new locations once completed" ([DarkZero](https://darkzero.co.uk/?p=1434)); the Sims Wiki calls them "Golden Wants" and notes only player 1 has them in two-player ([Sims Wiki](https://www.thesimswiki.com/wiki/The_Sims_2_(Xbox))).
- **Socialising = a dedicated mode.** "Social mode" shows an Interest Meter (0–8) and relationship score (-100 to 100); positive interactions chain into bonus points (manual). "The camera zooms in on their conversation and the player chooses their actions from a menu"; the background and Sim positions change with the relationship (lovers get a pink background) ([Sims Wiki](https://www.thesimswiki.com/wiki/The_Sims_2_(Xbox))). "The background blurs, and different colors represent different conversational tones" ([Worthplaying GC](https://worthplaying.com/article/2005/12/4/reviews/29444-gamecube-review-the-sims-2/)).
- **Interaction menus.** Direct Control: "left analogue stick and use the 'X' button to interact"; Classic: "left analogue stick works like a mouse cursor"; shoulder buttons "switch between objects, such as the radio or fridge, and fast-forward actions like sleeping" ([DarkZero](https://darkzero.co.uk/?p=1434)).
- **Food Creation.** Up to four ingredients from the fridge, prepared in processor, countertop or stove; combinations have effects (manual).
- **Buy/Build.** Categories (Seating, Tables, Entertainment, Plumbing, Miscellaneous); locked items unlock by fulfilling Wants; walls placed by setting two anchor points (manual).
- **Modes and extras.** Story mode and Freeplay share one save file (manual; also a complaint in [DarkZero](https://darkzero.co.uk/?p=1434)); two-player split-screen shares one family. Console-exclusive activities include a metal detector to find items, foosball, and a fiddle contest with the Grim Reaper ([Sims Wiki](https://www.thesimswiki.com/wiki/The_Sims_2_(Xbox))).
- **Reception.** Worthplaying's reviewer, given both control styles, "found myself sticking with the latter [point-and-click] much more often" ([Worthplaying](https://worthplaying.com/article/2005/12/4/reviews/29444-gamecube-review-the-sims-2/)). DarkZero: "the gameplay is really where the game shines", but invisible walls, load times and repetition ([DarkZero](https://darkzero.co.uk/?p=1434)).

### B3. The Sims 2 (PSP, 2005, Strangetown)

Source: [PSP manual, Europe](https://archive.org/details/psp-the-sims-2-europe) (OCR); [Sims Wiki PSP](https://www.thesimswiki.com/wiki/The_Sims_2_(PSP)).

- **Controls.** SELECT opens "Goals, My Sim and Inventory screens"; START opens the Main menu; "R button + L button" cycles camera position (manual). Circle opens Perks; L/R rotate the camera; Create-a-Sim uses D-pad, X to confirm, Triangle to cancel ([Sims Wiki](https://www.thesimswiki.com/wiki/The_Sims_2_(PSP))). Only one Sim is controlled; direct control throughout.
- **Sanity replaces Fears.** Sanity Meter colours: green happy, red "possible nervous breakdown", gold contented, platinum nearly full with urgencies ignored; fulfilling Wants raises Sanity and earns Sanity Points, Fears lower it, and it drops over time; in the red the Sim collapses and goes to a mental hospital (manual). GameSpot: "the game has a general 'sanity' meter that fills up when you accomplish wants and that empties out when you fail" **(search summary; [GameSpot](https://gamespot.com/reviews/the-sims-2-review/1900-6141125/))**.
- **The Social Game.** Three options at all times (chat, flirt, intimidate); after choosing, "watch the other Sim's reaction. An icon above their head shows what they expect next"; Charisma improves chat (manual). "The Sim you're talking to shows a speech bubble, and you press the button matching the icon shown or a closely related one" ([Sims Wiki](https://www.thesimswiki.com/wiki/The_Sims_2_(PSP))). Reception: a timed button system the reviewer likened to "a game of Snap", made worse by loading pauses "between sentences in dialogue scenes" ([NZ Herald](https://nzherald.co.nz/lifestyle/the-sims-2-psp/G77TBKBFVC7CCXC53AK43TRAP4)).
- **Secrets, Perks, Goals.** Each Sim has up to four secrets that can be kept, sold or traded; Goals come from NPCs and split into Tasks; Perks are bought with Sanity Points (manual; [Sims Wiki](https://www.thesimswiki.com/wiki/The_Sims_2_(PSP))).
- **Minigames (manual).** Smack-a-Ghoul (night only), Howlin' Hoedown line dance, Kine Sunrise Ritual (sync with others), a radio-telescope mimicry game, gardening, recycling, and "Artistic Expression" painting on an easel; some minigames create urgencies or lower Sanity.
- **Travel.** Walk to the map edge, "select the road map, then select a destination" (manual). Locations: Gas Station, Paradise Place, Deadtree, Division 47, The Monument (names partly inferred from garbled OCR).

### B4. The Sims 2: Pets (PS2/GameCube/Wii, 2006–07)

Source: [PS2 manual](https://archive.org/details/ps2_Sims_2_The-_Pets_USA) (OCR).

- **Controls.** Left stick "Move Sim/cursor"; right stick "Zoom/rotate camera"; SELECT toggles Classic/Direct; START pause. Direct Control is default: "Move the left analog stick to move your Sim towards an object you wish them to interact with", then press select and choose an action. Classic Control: a cursor queues actions and "when it touches an object, a menu appears if several are nearby"; holding a button and pressing the D-pad edits the action queue.
- **HUD.** Mood meter; Simoleons; time of day; "quick text/nearest pet wants"; Motives, Relationships, and Wants/Aspiration meter all on the D-pad; Personal Info under the pause menu.
- **Pet verbs are menu entries.** SCOLD (fills a learning bar over the pet's head), TEACH, TRY FOR OFFSPRING WITH, CHECK MICROCHIP under a Special menu. Town Square with carts and shops paid in pet points; ATM converts Simoleons.
- **Reception.** Nintendo Life: direct control "just like in any other third person game", with orders from above still available, but actions "fairly clunky and somewhat slow", "nothing close to mouse fluidity here"; 6/10 ([Nintendo Life GC](https://www.nintendolife.com/games/gamecube/sims_2_pets/review)). Cubed3: using a toilet means "scrolling down through the options, selecting 'Use'"; classic mode "so badly implemented that it is just about unusable"; 4/10 ([Cubed3 GC](https://www.cubed3.com/games/reviews/gamecube/the-sims-2-pets)). The Wii version's pointer "just simply don't feel as steady as they should" **(search summary; [Nintendo Life Wii](https://www.nintendolife.com/games/wii/sims_2_pets/review))**.

### B5. Design language shared by the console Sims

- Direct third-person control as default, classic cursor as a toggle (SELECT) ([Sims 2 PS2 manual](https://archive.org/details/ps2_Sims_2_The_USA); [Pets manual](https://archive.org/details/ps2_Sims_2_The-_Pets_USA)).
- Interaction = walk up, press action, pick from a short list; when several targets overlap, a disambiguation menu ([Pets manual](https://archive.org/details/ps2_Sims_2_The-_Pets_USA); [Gaming Nexus](https://www.gamingnexus.com/Article/439/The-Sims-Bustin-Out)).
- Needs/Wants/Relationships/Skills as panels on the four d-pad directions, not persistent on screen; mood meter, clock and money persistent ([Bustin' Out manual](https://archive.org/details/ps2_Sims_Bustin_Out_The_USA); [Sims 2 PS2 manual](https://archive.org/details/ps2_Sims_2_The_USA)).
- Shoulder buttons = time speed and Sim/object cycling ([Sims 2 PS2 manual](https://archive.org/details/ps2_Sims_2_The_USA); [DarkZero](https://darkzero.co.uk/?p=1434)).
- Conversation as a separate "Social mode" with a zoomed camera, a menu of options, a visible interest/reaction indicator and colour-coded tone ([Sims Wiki](https://www.thesimswiki.com/wiki/The_Sims_2_(Xbox)); [Worthplaying](https://worthplaying.com/article/2005/12/4/reviews/29444-gamecube-review-the-sims-2/)); on PSP, icon-prompted button matching ([PSP manual](https://archive.org/details/psp-the-sims-2-europe)).
- Inventory under the pause menu ("FAMILY INVENTORY") or SELECT ("Pockets" on PSP) ([Pets manual](https://archive.org/details/ps2_Sims_2_The-_Pets_USA); [PSP manual](https://archive.org/details/psp-the-sims-2-europe)).
- No pie menus are described in any manual or review found; lists and category menus throughout.

## Part C — Synthesis: patterns and anti-patterns for our redesign

### Patterns that worked

1. **Merged widescreen view by default, "classic" layout as an option.** Precedents: Pokémon FullDex MERGED mode ([README](https://github.com/mamesj/pokemon-fulldex)); Dominus layouts switchable in the pause menu ([Nintendo Everything](https://nintendoeverything.com/review-castlevania-dominus-collection-switch/)); DeSmuME hybrid/top with "Show both screens" ([libretro](https://docs.libretro.com/library/desmume/)). Applied: town free roam fills 16:9; keep a selectable two-panel layout for purists and for debugging.

2. **Promote persistent lower-screen info to a corner overlay with a one-button full view, and fade it during action.** Precedent: Luigi's Mansion 2 HD minimap in the top-right, maximised with '-' and faded while using the Poltergust ([My Nintendo News](https://mynintendonews.com/2024/06/11/preview-luigis-mansion-2-hd/)); FullDex hides the Pokétch and keeps it one press away ([README](https://github.com/mamesj/pokemon-fulldex)). Applied: mood/clock/money persistent; the DS bottom-screen needs bars become a compact corner widget; full panel on a button.

3. **Tab panels on the d-pad.** Precedent: Bustin' Out "D-button left opens the Career panel, right opens Relationship, down opens Motives, and up opens Personality" ([manual](https://archive.org/details/ps2_Sims_Bustin_Out_The_USA)); Sims 2 and Pets put Needs/Skills/Wants/Relationships on the d-pad ([Sims 2 manual](https://archive.org/details/ps2_Sims_2_The_USA); [Pets manual](https://archive.org/details/ps2_Sims_2_The-_Pets_USA)). Applied: our tab menus map directly to d-pad directions; L/R cycles tabs once a panel is open.

4. **Walk-up action button with a short list, plus disambiguation when targets overlap.** Precedent: Pets Direct Control ([manual](https://archive.org/details/ps2_Sims_2_The-_Pets_USA)); Bustin' Out's pop-up listing "the fridge and the name of the person standing near it" ([Gaming Nexus](https://www.gamingnexus.com/Article/439/The-Sims-Bustin-Out)); AAI "control stick or D-pad to move Miles" then press on things ([NWR](https://nintendoworldreport.com/review/68426)). Applied: free roam interactions; keep lists to a handful of verbs; no cursor on the main loop.

5. **Shoulder buttons for time and target cycling.** Precedent: L1/R1 freeze/fast-forward, L2/R2 switch Sim ([Sims 2 manual](https://archive.org/details/ps2_Sims_2_The_USA)); shoulder buttons "switch between objects, such as the radio or fridge" ([DarkZero](https://darkzero.co.uk/?p=1434)). Applied: cycle nearby interactables and NPCs in town without aiming.

6. **Dialogue as a dedicated Social mode with a visible reaction indicator.** Precedent: Sims 2 console Interest Meter and zoomed camera ([manual](https://archive.org/details/ps2_Sims_2_The_USA); [Sims Wiki](https://www.thesimswiki.com/wiki/The_Sims_2_(Xbox))); colour-coded tone with blurred background ([Worthplaying](https://worthplaying.com/article/2005/12/4/reviews/29444-gamecube-review-the-sims-2/)); PSP icon-above-head prompt ([PSP manual](https://archive.org/details/psp-the-sims-2-europe)). Applied: dialogue and social reactions use a reaction icon over the NPC and a short option list; untimed by default (see anti-pattern 5).

7. **Gesture minigames → discrete button patterns, original gesture kept as an optional path, with rewind/retry.** Precedent: Dominus Magic Seals "each seal has a pattern of button inputs" with tracing still available and rewind "perfect if you flub a magic seal input" ([CGMagazine](https://www.cgmagonline.com/review/game/castlevania-dominus-collection-ps5/); [Nintendo Everything](https://nintendoeverything.com/review-castlevania-dominus-collection-switch/)). Applied: vacuum (stick sweep + button hold), cards (button-select), music mixer (face buttons per track, sticks for faders), each with instant retry.

8. **Virtual cursor on the right stick with trigger-to-tap, only for inherently spatial tasks, with examined-area feedback.** Precedent: Dominus cursor ([CGMagazine](https://www.cgmagonline.com/review/game/castlevania-dominus-collection-ps5/)); Phoenix Wright's magnifying glass that "does inform players where they've already examined" ([Hardcore Gamer](https://hardcoregamer.com/reviews/review-phoenix-wright-ace-attorney-trilogy-console/328566/)); DeSmuME/melonDS libretro right-stick pointer ([DeSmuME](https://docs.libretro.com/library/desmume/); [melonDS DS](https://docs.libretro.com/library/melonds_ds/)). Applied: painting/drawing and metal detector only; show swept/scanned areas; offer two or three cursor speeds.

9. **Line-trace by stick where the DS used a stylus stroke.** Precedent: Ghost Trick's line to an object's core by "moving the joystick" ([NWR](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/64148)). Applied: metal detector sweeps and any drag-to-target interactions become stick direction + hold.

10. **Keep touch on Switch handheld, but design buttons as the primary path and let touch be toggled.** Precedent: Picross S7 "Touch Hold" / "Touch Toggle" ([NWR](https://nintendoworldreport.com/review/59283/picross-s7-switch-review)); Etrian "either the touch screen or traditional controls" ([Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/reviews/switch-eshop/etrian-odyssey-origins-collection)); Ghost Trick on Switch supports both ([Nintendo UK](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/Games/Nintendo-Switch-games/Game-2338699.html)). Applied: painting gets optional touch in handheld; nothing requires it.

11. **Assist and skip options for adapted mechanics.** Precedent: Ace Attorney Story Mode, consult, notes, text log ([Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/reviews/nintendo-switch/apollo-justice-ace-attorney-trilogy); [Vooks](https://vooks.net/ace-attorney-investigations-collection-switch-review)); Etrian automap ([Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/reviews/switch-eshop/etrian-odyssey-origins-collection)). Applied: per-minigame "auto-complete at reduced reward" and a task log for Goals.

12. **Decide the aspect ratio deliberately and widen the world.** Precedent: Ghost Trick kept 4:3 and was asked for 16:9 ([RPGFan](https://www.rpgfan.com/review/ghost-trick-phantom-detective-remaster/)); FullDex's "WIDESCREEN WORLD" extends scenery to the edges ([README](https://github.com/mamesj/pokemon-fulldex)). Applied: free roam in true 16:9, UI in the margins rather than letterboxing.

### Anti-patterns

1. **Pointer or motion emulation as the main input.** TWEWY Final Remix docked: "manageable, but far from intuitive", "neither feels like a completely satisfying option" ([Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/reviews/nintendo-switch/world_ends_with_you_final_remix)); Spirit Tracks on Wii U: "fiddly compromise" ([Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/games/ds/legend_of_zelda_spirit_tracks/review)); Layton d-pad cursor with "only two speeds" ([Cubed3](https://www.cubed3.com/games/reviews/nintendo-switch/laytons-mystery-journey-katrielle-and-the-millionaires-conspiracy-deluxe-edition)). Do not drive town navigation or dialogue with a cursor.

2. **Literal reproduction of both screens that shrinks the information.** Dominus handheld map "quite small by default" ([NWR](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/68577)); Layton HD mobile's split screen and finger precision ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/professor-layton-on-mobile-could-really-use-a-stylus-o-1829401026)); Layton Deluxe memo "now covers the puzzle explanation" ([Cubed3](https://www.cubed3.com/games/reviews/nintendo-switch/laytons-mystery-journey-katrielle-and-the-millionaires-conspiracy-deluxe-edition)). Re-flow content; do not scale the bottom screen down and bolt it on.

3. **Long scrolling lists and slow cursors for everyday actions.** Pets: "scrolling down through the options, selecting 'Use'" ([Cubed3](https://www.cubed3.com/games/reviews/gamecube/the-sims-2-pets)); "nothing close to mouse fluidity here" ([Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/games/gamecube/sims_2_pets/review)); Phoenix Wright "the speed of the cursor" ([Hardcore Gamer](https://hardcoregamer.com/reviews/review-phoenix-wright-ace-attorney-trilogy-console/328566/)). Default verb on first press; lists capped; cursor speed adjustable.

4. **Removing a touch mechanic and leaving a hollow button press.** Apollo Justice forensics "replaced by simple button presses that leave them feeling a bit vestigial" ([RPG Site](https://rpgsite.net/review/15365-apollo-justice-ace-attorney-trilogy-eview)); Another Code dropping its puzzles ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/gaming/another-code-recollection-review)). Each of our five minigames needs a real controller mechanic, not a confirm prompt.

5. **Timed button-matching conversations and hidden extras.** Sims 2 PSP's social game felt like "a game of Snap" with loading pauses between lines ([NZ Herald](https://nzherald.co.nz/lifestyle/the-sims-2-psp/G77TBKBFVC7CCXC53AK43TRAP4)); Bravely Default's new minigames "hidden in submenus" ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/gaming/nintendo/bravely-default-flying-fairy-hd-remaster-review)). Keep dialogue untimed by default and surface minigames in the world, not in menus.
